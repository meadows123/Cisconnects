## Why "Installed" Doesn't Mean "Optimised"

There are three reasons a properly installed WiFi network can still underperform, and they're not problems you'll solve by upgrading your broadband package.

### 1. The router is in the wrong place

This is the single most common cause of uneven WiFi coverage, and it's more common than most people expect, even in setups that were professionally installed.

WiFi signal radiates outward from the access point. Place the AP near the front of a long narrow office and the back half gets weak signal. Mount it in a corner and you're wasting half the signal on the wall behind it. Put it on the floor behind a desk and furniture absorption destroys the coverage footprint.

Optimal AP placement puts the access point centrally to the area it's covering, at ceiling height, away from metal objects and interference sources. When this isn't right, no amount of configuration fixes it.

One of our customers, Sam, a home office user, was getting 18Mbps on WiFi when his broadband was 200Mbps. His router was in the wrong room, on the wrong side of the house. One visit, repositioned equipment, and he's now getting the full 200Mbps where he works. Same router. Same broadband. Different result.

### 2. Interference is degrading your signal

Your WiFi doesn't operate in isolation. It shares the 2.4GHz and 5GHz bands with every other wireless network in range, your neighbours' routers, the café across the street, the Bluetooth devices in your office, the cordless phones, the microwave in the kitchen.

When multiple networks share the same channel, they create co-channel interference that degrades performance for everyone. The symptoms: intermittent disconnections, speeds that are inconsistently slow, devices that show full signal bars but still feel sluggish.

Ben, a small business owner, had loads of interference from neighbouring networks and his router placed in completely the wrong position. After a single optimisation visit, speeds tripled, not because the hardware changed, but because the interference channels were identified and the placement was fixed.

Interference analysis requires proper wireless scanning equipment, the kind that shows you exactly what channels the surrounding networks are using and what your AP should be on to minimise conflict. This isn't something you can diagnose from a speed test.

### 3. The coverage design doesn't match how your space is actually used

WiFi coverage that looked right during installation may not match how the space has evolved.

You've rearranged the furniture. Added a partition. Started using the back room as a second meeting space. Moved to a different floor. Added 10 more staff. Switched from desktop PCs to laptops and moved around more.

Every change to how your space is used can create gaps between where coverage exists and where it's needed. An optimisation visit reassesses your actual usage against your current coverage, and adjusts placement, adds access points where needed, or reconfigures settings to match the reality.

Anna, a retail store owner, had half her shop with no WiFi. She couldn't process card payments from the floor. One access point added to the right location, configured properly, and the problem was gone. Not a system replacement. Just the right fix in the right place.

## The Difference Between Installation and Optimisation

Installation is about getting the infrastructure in. Optimisation is about making that infrastructure perform.

| | Installation | Optimisation |
|---|---|---|
| **Focus** | Getting the hardware in and connected | Getting the hardware performing for your space |
| **Starting point** | Empty or new space | Existing network with real-world issues |
| **Process** | Survey, design, cable, configure, test | Diagnose, adjust, verify |
| **Outcome** | Network is operational | Network performs across the whole space |
| **When** | New office, new build, major upgrade | After installation, when problems appear, as space evolves |

Most businesses need both at some point. Installation creates the foundation. Optimisation is what ensures it stays performing as the environment changes around it.

## What Professional WiFi Optimisation Actually Involves

Professional WiFi optimisation isn't a man coming to look at your router and leaving. It's a structured process that identifies exactly what's causing your problems and fixes them.

**Step 1: Wireless analysis**
A proper wireless survey of your space, measuring signal strength across every area, scanning for interference sources, identifying which channels your surrounding networks are using, and mapping where coverage is strong versus weak. This gives an accurate picture of what's actually happening, not what should be happening based on the installation plan.

**Step 2: Diagnosis**
Identifying the specific causes of your performance issues. Is it placement? Interference? Insufficient access points? Configuration that's drifted from optimal? Client devices that are "sticky", holding onto a distant AP when a closer one is available? Each problem has a different fix.

**Step 3: Remediation**
Making the changes, repositioning APs where needed, adding hardware where gaps can't be closed by repositioning, reconfiguring channel allocation to minimise interference, adjusting power settings, enabling band steering to push capable devices to 5GHz, setting QoS rules to prioritise important traffic.

**Step 4: Verification**
Testing the changes under real conditions, with devices in the problem areas, with the application types that were causing issues. The outcome should be measurable: signal strength improved, speed test results up, dropped connections gone.

This is what separates professional optimisation from "have you tried turning it off and on again."

## Signs Your Business WiFi Needs Optimisation

If any of these sound familiar, you're experiencing a fixable problem, not a fundamental limitation of your infrastructure:

- Signal is strong near the router but weak in certain rooms or areas
- Speeds are inconsistent, sometimes fast, sometimes slow, for no obvious reason
- Devices drop connection and reconnect frequently
- Video calls work fine from some desks but poorly from others
- Staff know which spots to avoid for important calls
- A WiFi extender was added and somehow made things worse
- Your broadband test results are fast but your WiFi feels slow
- One area (stockroom, back office, upstairs) has noticeably worse performance

These are all symptoms with causes that can be diagnosed and fixed. They're not signs that your broadband package is too slow or your hardware needs replacing.
