## The Real Reasons Network Automation Fails


Let’s be honest: it’s rarely about the technology. Here’s what really gets in the way:

> **External Resource:** [Cisco’s Network Automation Solutions](https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/solutions/enterprise-networks/network-automation/index.html)

### 1. Siloed Teams and Knowledge
- Network, security, and cloud teams all have their own tools and processes.
- Automation efforts get stuck in one group, never reaching the whole org.

### 2. Lack of Buy-In (or Fear of Change)
- Engineers worry automation will break things—or make their jobs obsolete.
- Leadership wants results, but doesn’t invest in training or process change.

### 3. Complexity and Sprawl
- Too many tools, too many scripts, not enough standardization.
- No single source of truth for configs, changes, or troubleshooting.

### 4. No Closed Loop
- Automation runs, but nobody validates the outcome.
- Manual checks creep back in, and trust in automation erodes.

### 5. Short-Term Thinking
- Quick wins are celebrated, but there’s no long-term strategy.
- Automation is treated as a project, not a core capability.

The answer isn’t just “more tools.” It’s a new way of thinking about automation:

![Team collaborating on a network automation strategy](https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1519389950473-47c0e7f7c2b9?w=1200&h=600&fit=crop)

### 1. Orchestrate, Don’t Just Automate
- Connect your tools, teams, and processes.
- Use orchestration platforms to manage workflows end-to-end.

### 2. Validate Everything
- Build in automated checks and closed-loop feedback.
- Trust, but verify—every change, every time.

### 3. Make Automation a Team Sport
- Break down silos with shared playbooks and cross-team training.
- Celebrate wins and learn from failures—together.

### 4. Think Long-Term
- Treat automation as a journey, not a one-off project.
- Invest in documentation, version control, and continuous improvement.
