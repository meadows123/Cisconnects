# AIOps Benefits by Team: What NetOps, SecOps and Leadership Each Get

AIOps means something different depending on who in the organisation you ask. The engineer on call cares about fewer 2am pages. The team manager cares about MTTR and retention. The budget holder cares about a number that justifies the spend. Pitching the same generic benefit list to all three is why AIOps proposals often stall in the room, the person reading it can't find the part that's about them.

Our guide to [AIOps benefits and use cases](/blog/aiops-benefits-and-use-cases) covers the core benefits broadly. This guide breaks them down by who actually feels each one, and how to pitch the platform to each stakeholder in the terms they care about.

## For Network and IT Operations Engineers

**What changes day to day:** far fewer low-value alerts landing in the queue, because deduplication, correlation, and dynamic baselining have already filtered out the noise before it reaches a person. When something does need attention, root cause analysis starts from a probable cause instead of a wall of dashboards to cross-reference manually. Our guide to [reducing alert fatigue](/blog/how-to-reduce-alert-fatigue-it-operations) covers exactly what this looks like in practice.

**What it means for on-call.** Fewer pages for things that turn out to be nothing. The pages that do come through carry more context, what changed recently, what's the likely cause, rather than a bare threshold breach with no story attached.

**How to pitch it to this group specifically:** don't lead with dashboards or ROI. Lead with the actual change to their week, less noise, less 2am firefighting for non-issues, more of their time going toward the design and improvement work that's usually the first thing to get deprioritised when the team is firefighting.

## For Team Leads and Operations Managers

**What changes for this role:** the metrics you're accountable for move in the right direction, mean time to detect, mean time to resolve, and the ratio of proactive to reactive work your team is actually doing. Staffing pressure eases slightly too. Difficulty hiring skilled network technology experts has risen from 26% of organisations in 2022 to 52% today, per EMA's research, and a platform that reduces how much specialist manual investigation a given incident requires reduces how exposed your team is to that hiring gap.

**What it means for retention.** Alert fatigue and constant on-call burden are among the most reliably cited reasons experienced operations engineers leave a role. A visible reduction in noise and after-hours pages is a retention lever, not just an efficiency one.

**How to pitch it to this group specifically:** frame it around the metrics they already report upward, MTTR, incident volume, and the skills-gap exposure the team currently carries, rather than the technical mechanics of correlation and baselining.

## For Security Teams

**What changes for SecOps specifically:** the same correlation and noise-reduction principles apply to security telemetry, and the scale of the problem is often worse than network operations, 46% of security alerts are false positives and 76% of organisations cite alert fatigue as a primary SOC concern. Our guide to [AIOps for security operations](/blog/aiops-use-cases-security-operations) covers this use case in full.

**What it means day to day.** Analysts start a shift with a prioritised, correlated set of genuine concerns instead of a queue of several thousand raw alerts, most producing no real security value.

**How to pitch it to this group specifically:** be clear this isn't a SIEM or SOAR replacement, it's noise reduction and correlation applied consistently across whatever telemetry sources already feed those tools.

## For the CIO or Budget Holder

**What changes for this stakeholder:** a defensible number. The real cost being addressed isn't a line item today, it's engineer hours spent on manual triage, the cost of downtime that ran longer than it should have, and the cost of replacing burned-out staff, none of which show up clearly on a current budget. Our guide to [calculating AIOps ROI](/blog/aiops-roi-business-case) walks through building that comparison with real numbers rather than vendor benchmarks.

**What it means for risk, not just cost.** Faster detection and more consistent, auditable remediation reduce the tail risk of a major outage or security incident, which is a harder number to put in a spreadsheet but a real part of the case, especially for regulated or customer-facing environments where [downtime carries a direct, measurable cost](/blog/reduce-network-downtime).

**How to pitch it to this group specifically:** lead with the payback calculation, not the feature list. A budget holder approves a number that makes sense against a comparison they trust, not a description of what the platform technically does.

## For the Wider Business

**What changes beyond IT.** Fewer and shorter outages mean fewer customer-facing incidents, which matters directly for revenue in any business where uptime is part of the product experience. Faster, more consistent incident response also matters for any team relying on IT and network stability to do their own job, sales during a demo, finance during a close, anyone on a customer call.

## How to Pitch AIOps to a Mixed Room

Most real approval conversations involve more than one of these stakeholders at once. The approach that works: open with the CIO-level number, the current estimated cost of the gap, then walk through what changes for the operations team specifically, since that's usually where the most credible, ground-level evidence for the number comes from. Close with the proposed first use case and its measurable before-and-after metric, not a full rollout ask. Our guide to [choosing your first AIOps use case](/blog/choosing-your-first-aiops-use-case) covers picking that scoped starting point.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who should own an AIOps rollout internally?

Usually network or IT operations leadership, since they're closest to the day-to-day pain and the data needed to build the business case. Security stakeholders should be involved early if security use cases are part of the roadmap, since the data sources and ownership differ from network operations.

### Does AIOps benefit small teams the same way as large ones?

The mechanism is the same, but the scale of benefit tends to track environment complexity and alert volume more than team size specifically. A small team managing a large, complex, fast-changing environment often sees more benefit than a larger team managing something simple and stable.

### How do we get buy-in from engineers who are sceptical of "another tool"?

Involve them in scoring the first use case rather than presenting a decision already made, and start with the use case that removes pain they already feel daily, alert noise, rather than one that sounds impressive to leadership but doesn't change their week.
